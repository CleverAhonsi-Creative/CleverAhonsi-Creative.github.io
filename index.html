S-7

Average vs typical: mean and median

STATISTICS
The maths

Mean = add everything, divide by the count: 36 ÷ 9 = 4 days. Median = the middle value when sorted: 2 days.

In plain words

One graphic stuck for 18 days drags the average to 4. The median says most graphics clear in about 2 days.

Why it matters

Report the median for "typical turnaround" and treat the outlier as its own problem to fix.

S-8

The bell curve and the 68–95–99.7 rule

STATISTICS
The maths

Standard deviation (SD) measures spread around the average. Here the average is 6.0 h and the SD is 0.4 h.

In plain words

About 68 in 100 sorties land within one SD (5.6–6.4 h). About 95 in 100 land within two SD.

Why it matters

"6 hours on station" hides the spread. "5.2 h or more in 97 of 100 sorties" tells the reader what to plan around.

S-9

Why backup links multiply reliability

PROBABILITY
The maths

If one link fails 10% of the time, independent links all fail together with probability 0.1 × 0.1 × … So: 10%, then 1%, then 0.1%.

In plain words

Each orange square is a sortie that loses contact completely. Adding one backup turns ten bad days into one.

The catch

This only holds if the links fail independently. Two radios jammed by the same signal count as one link.

S-10

Which opportunity to chase: expected value

DECISION MATHS
The maths

Expected value = chance of success × value − cost to pursue. A: 0.30 × $10M − $0.4M = $2.6M. B: 0.05 × $40M − $1.2M = $0.8M.

In plain words

The bigger deal is worth less to chase, because the odds are low and pursuing it is expensive.

Why it matters

It turns a gut call into one comparable number per option, so a team can decide where its time goes.
